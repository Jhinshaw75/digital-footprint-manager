About This Guide & Maintenance Schedule

What is the purpose of this guide? This tool is designed to help you regain control of your personal privacy. Over time, personal details like your home address, phone number, and email accumulate across public directories and corporate data breaches without your knowledge.

How does it protect you? By following these simple steps, you will:

  • Block Scammers & Fraudsters: Remove your phone numbers and family histories from public search sites so scammers cannot use them to impersonate government agents or run "grandparent scams."
  • Secure Your Accounts: Lock down your passwords with an encrypted manager and stop hidden web trackers from monitoring your browsing habits.
  • Hide Your Real Inbox: Use free email masking so promotional sign-ups and corporate data leaks never flood your main email account.
  • Scrub Hidden Location Data: Remove secret GPS coordinates and disable future geotagging on family photos before you text or email them online.

Recommended Maintenance Schedule: Phases 1 and 2 require periodic 90-day maintenance checks because public records and data brokers continuously re-scrape information. Phases 3, 4, and 5 are one-time permanent setups that do not need to be repeated.

Phase 1: Check Where You Show Up

🔄 90-Day Maintenance Cycle
What is Have I Been Pwned? A trusted, secure public safety directory that tracks major corporate data breaches. Checking your email here lets you know immediately if an online store, doctor's office, or website you use was hacked so you can change your password before a scammer tries to log in.
Safety Check
  1. Check Your Email Address
    Go to Have I Been Pwned, type in your email address, and press enter to see if your information appeared in any past company data leaks.
  2. See What Was Exposed
    Look to see if things like old passwords, phone numbers, or home addresses showed up in those leaks.
  3. Update Your Passwords
    If an account was affected, change your password immediately. Always use a different password for every website so that if one place gets hacked, your other accounts stay safe.
  4. Hide Your Email on the Public List
    Go to the HIBP Opt-Out Page, enter your email, check your inbox for a confirmation message, click the link inside, and choose to hide your email from public searches.

Phase 2: Remove Yourself From People-Search Websites

🔄 90-Day Maintenance Cycle
Important Note: These websites often try to scare you into paying for a monthly subscription service (like Onerep). Ignore and close those offers! Removing your information manually using these free guides costs $0.
📱 Mobile Device Tip: If an external opt-out page on your phone freezes or blocks you from tapping a security check (captcha), open this guide on a desktop/laptop computer, or use the direct email fallback listed below.

1. TruePeopleSearch

What it is & Scam Risk: A public directory publishing names, ages, phone numbers, and home histories. Scammers search this site to gather personal details to impersonate government agents or utility companies over the phone.
View Step-by-Step Removal Guide
  1. Go to the TruePeopleSearch Opt-Out Portal.
  2. Type in your email address, check the box proving you are human, and submit the form. (Note: If the mobile captcha box is unclickable, open the link on a computer or laptop).
  3. Open your email inbox, look for the verification message, and click the confirmation link inside.
  4. Fill out your name and address details to match the specific public record you want removed.
  5. Submit the final form. Your listing will automatically disappear within about 3 days.
Alternative Support Contact: If the web form fails on your mobile device, you can email TruePeopleSearch directly at support@truepeoplesearch.com with your full name, city/state, profile URL, and phone number to request manual removal.

2. FastPeopleSearch

What it is & Scam Risk: A directory compiling deep background records and contact data. Scammers use it to look up relatives' names so they can run "grandparent scams" pretending a family member is in an emergency.
📱 Mobile Device Tip: If an external opt-out page on your phone freezes or blocks you from tapping a security check, open this guide on a desktop/laptop computer, or use the direct email fallback listed below.
View Step-by-Step Removal Guide
  1. Go to the FastPeopleSearch Opt-Out Portal.
  2. Enter your name and email address, then submit the starting form. (Note: If the mobile verification box is unclickable, open the link on a computer or laptop).
  3. Open your email inbox for the verification message and click the link inside.
  4. Fill out the quick identity form matching your personal details and address.
  5. Click submit to lock in your removal request.
Alternative Support Contact: If the web form fails on your mobile device, you can email FastPeopleSearch support directly at support@fastpeoplesearch.com to request manual removal.

3. Whitepages

What it is & Scam Risk: A long-standing phone and address lookup directory. Fraudsters use it to cross-reference addresses and landlines to send targeted physical mail scams or fraudulent sweepstakes offers.
📱 Mobile Device Tip: If verification forms freeze or fail to register taps on your phone, open this guide on a desktop/laptop computer for smoother navigation.
View Step-by-Step Removal Guide
  1. Search for your name on Whitepages.com, accept welcome pop-ups, and open up your specific profile's "View Details" page.
  2. Highlight and copy the website link (URL) from the very top bar of your internet browser.
  3. Go to the Whitepages Suppression Requests Page and paste your profile link into the box. (Note: If fields do not register properly on mobile, complete this step on a computer).
  4. Confirm your details and follow their instructions (which may include a quick automated phone call) to finish taking down the listing.

4. Spokeo

What it is & Scam Risk: A data aggregator that links public records, social media profiles, and wealth estimates. Scammers use it to target wealthier profiles or seniors living alone for financial fraud.
📱 Mobile Device Tip: Spokeo's picture puzzle (CAPTCHA) can be difficult to tap accurately on mobile screens. If it fails to submit, open this link on a computer or laptop.
View Step-by-Step Removal Guide
  1. Navigate to the official Spokeo Opt-Out Page.
  2. Search for your name on Spokeo in a separate tab, click on your profile card, and copy the link from your browser's top address bar.
  3. Go back to the Spokeo Opt-Out page, paste your link, type your email, complete the picture puzzle (CAPTCHA), and click Opt Out. (Note: Complete on a PC if the puzzle refuses to load).
  4. Open your email inbox, find the confirmation message from Spokeo, and click the link inside to complete your removal.

Phase 3: Secure Your Web Browser & Passwords

🛠️ One-Time Permanent Setup
Why do this? Hackers and advertisers use hidden tracking scripts and weak passwords to access personal accounts. These free tools help block those trackers and lock your accounts behind a secure vault so you don't have to memorize dozens of passwords.

1. uBlock Origin (Ad Blocker)

What it is & Scam Risk: A browser extension that blocks malicious pop-ups and tracking scripts. Without it, fraudulent banner ads can trick users into clicking fake tech-support warning windows.
View Step-by-Step Installation Guide
  1. Go to the official uBlock Origin Website.
  2. Click the red "Get uBlock Origin" button to add this 100% free helper tool to your web browser.
  3. Confirm the installation prompt when your browser asks if you want to add the extension. It will now quietly block invisible trackers and annoying pop-up ads before they load.

2. Bitwarden (Password Vault)

What it is & Scam Risk: A secure password vault that keeps login credentials encrypted. Leaving passwords written on paper near a computer allows anyone physical access to compromise banking and email accounts.
View Step-by-Step Setup Guide
  1. Go to the official Bitwarden Website to set up a free password manager account.
  2. Create a strong, secure "Master Password" that only you know. This is the only password you will ever need to memorize.
  3. Install the browser extension or app so it can safely store all your complex passwords and fill them out automatically.

3. Two-Step Login (Extra Security Lock)

What it is & Scam Risk: An extra security layer requiring a phone code plus your password. If a scammer steals your password via phishing, 2FA blocks them from breaking into your account without your phone.
View Setup Instructions

For important accounts like email and banking, go into your account settings and turn on "Two-Step Verification". This adds a deadbolt lock by texting a quick code to your phone when you log in.

If you use a Google account, manage this via the Google 2-Step Verification Guide.

Phase 4: Mask Your Email Address

🛠️ One-Time Permanent Setup
Why do this? Whenever you sign up for store discounts, newsletters, or online accounts, giving out your real email address opens you up to endless spam and risk if that company gets hacked. Email "masking" creates a temporary, private forwarding address so your real inbox stays completely hidden and clean.

DuckDuckGo Email Protection (Private Email Forwarder)

View Step-by-Step Setup Guide
  1. Visit the official DuckDuckGo Email Protection Page. This service is 100% free with no hidden fees or subscriptions.
  2. Click to get started, choose a personal username (which creates your core @duck.com address), and enter your real email address so it knows where to forward your messages.
  3. Whenever a website asks for your email address and you want to keep your real one private, use your custom @duck.com address instead. Important messages will automatically forward safely right to your regular inbox!

Phase 5: Clean Hidden Location Data From Photos

🛠️ One-Time Permanent Setup
Why do this? When you take a picture with a smartphone, the file often saves invisible hidden details (called EXIF data) like the exact GPS coordinates where the photo was snapped, the date, and what device you used. Cleaning this data prevents strangers online from tracking your physical location or habits from shared pictures.

Photo Metadata Scrubbing & Future Geotagging Control (Location Cleaner)

View Device Instructions

Choose your device below to remove existing location metadata and configure settings to prevent future geotagging:

📁 Windows PC (Windows 10 / 11)
  1. Open your computer's folder and find the photo or document you want to share online.
  2. Right-click on the file icon with your mouse and click on Properties at the very bottom of the menu.
  3. Click on the Details tab at the top of that window, click the blue link at the bottom that says "Remove Properties and Personal Information", check the box to remove all items from the file, and click OK.
🍏 Apple Mac (macOS)

Apple computers generally do not embed visible GPS data in standard shared images the same way Windows does, but you can always open photos in the built-in Preview app, click Tools > Adjust Color or export as a fresh copy to strip hidden metadata.

📱 iPhone / iPad (iOS)
  1. To strip location from an existing photo before sharing: Open your Photos app, tap the picture, tap the Share button, tap "Options" at the top, and toggle OFF the switch for Location.
  2. To stop future photos from recording GPS coordinates: Open Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > Camera and select "Never".
🤖 Android Phone (Samsung, Google Pixel, etc.)
  1. To strip location from an existing photo: Open your Gallery or Google Photos app, select the picture, swipe up or tap the three dots for details, and tap "Edit" next to location data to remove it.
  2. To stop future photos from saving location: Open your Camera app settings, locate "Save location" or "Location tags", and switch it OFF.
